Cloud computing is a model for enabling convenient, on-demand network access to a shared
pool of configurable computing resources. The resources can be rapidly provisioned and
released with minimal management effort.

Five characteristics are usually named. On-demand self-service means a consumer can obtain
capacity without human interaction with the provider. Broad network access means the
capacity is reached over the network by standard mechanisms. Resource pooling means the
provider serves many consumers from the same physical hardware. Rapid elasticity means
capacity can grow and shrink quickly, and can appear unlimited to the consumer. Measured
service means usage is metered, and therefore billed.

Metering is the characteristic students underestimate. A resource that is provisioned is
metered whether or not anyone is using it. This is why an idle virtual machine still costs
money, and why a reserved address that is attached to nothing can cost more than one doing
useful work.
